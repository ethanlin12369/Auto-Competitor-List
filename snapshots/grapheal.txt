Graphene is the next-generation material for sensing.
Awarded the Nobel Prize in 2010, graphene has given rise to a new field of science (2D nanoelectronics) which is already opening up astonishing new possibilities for a frugal and revolutionary technology. As a two-dimensional, i.e. a volume-less material, electrical currents are forced to flow across graphene surface, optimizing their coupling with the environment, leading to high-precision, versatile sensors that can accurately target trace levels of multiple analytes such as contaminants or pathogens simultaneously.

Graphene on polymer
Monolayer graphene is functionalized to become sensitive to a specific target. Then, when it detects changes in the target, its electric conductivity is modified.
Electrodes
The printed electrodes detect this change of conductivity
and translate it into an electrical analog current signal.
System-on-Chip (SoC)
The System-on-Chip computes the sensors signals: it makes
the data digital and store them.
Antenna
The antenna enables wireless communication
& harvest energy to power the sensors.

GRAPHEAL SECURES €2.5M FROM EIC ACCELERATOR PROGRAM TO DEVELOP FAST, FIELD-DEPLOYABLE GRAPHENE SENSORS FOR ULTRA-TRACE, REAL-TIME PFAS DETECTION
PFAS, known as “forever chemicals,” are increasingly detected across the world and are to date impossible to detect in the field. Our device PFAST is the deep tech answer to this problem. This June marks for Grapheal , the kick-off month of the EIC Accelerator Grant from the European Innovation Council supporting PFAST. By enabling easy testing at point of need, our sensing platform could allow for more frequent PFAS monitoring as required by new 2026 EU Drinking Water Directive. See more
